The Idea

About the Research

33 Koti is an exploratory project by Arpit Pareek asking whether the traditional 33 categories of Devas can be read as recurring inner patterns — qualities that appear across individuals, families, generations and regions, changing and combining as they travel.

It began during the COVID-19 lockdown, in conversations with friends from different states of India. Some qualities kept reappearing in different families and generations. That curiosity became this study.

Inner core here means recurring and spontaneous thoughts, subconscious and emotional tendencies, intuition, choices and reactions — not outer appearance. Category ≠ Person. A category is only a possible pattern of qualities.

The project does not read 33 Koti as 33 crore humans, and it keeps tradition, personal hypothesis and empirical investigation separate. It also asks the hardest question: “What evidence would show that I am wrong?”

Traditional Framework

The 33 Categories

The Vedic tradition (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad and Puranas) counts 33 devas: 12 Adityas, 8 Vasus, 11 Rudras and 2 Ashvins. The Bhagavad Gita (ch. 10) names Vishnu among the Adityas, Shankara among the Rudras and Pavaka among the Vasus. Some texts swap in Indra and Prajapati for the Ashvins, and name lists vary. Qualities below are traditional associations, not proven human types.

Honesty First

Methodology

Pipeline

Categories → traditional qualities → operational definitions → interviews and observations → individual profiles → family and generation comparison → state comparison → analysis.

Bias Checks

Confirmation bias is the main risk. Ambiguous cases, multiple categories and contradictions will be recorded openly.

Other Explanations

Upbringing, culture, environment, occupation, personality, genetics and chance will be weighed against the hypothesis.

Limits

Data is self-reported and unverified. This is a hypothesis, not scientific proof.
